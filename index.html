Year 1 carries the launch, the identity-check setup fee and the whole acquisition ramp across just 1,247 paying subscribers. It is negative at every price we modelled.

What is left is not profit, and it is not even revenue. Two things sit between this page and money in the bank. The app-store cut is excluded entirely, by instruction, and is a later exercise. And salaries, product development, customer support and office costs are excluded, as they were in the published model. A team of six at Malaysian market rates is roughly RM 1.5m–2.0m over three years.

The threshold that matters is a user base, not a revenue number. Roughly 25,000 monthly active users. Below 10,000, no configuration in this analysis works at any price. That is also the point at which the top ladder is worth revisiting.

How confident is this? The arithmetic is verified — the model was rebuilt independently in Python and every figure reconciles, under 79 automated checks. The inputs are a mix. Vendor rates, cloud costs and the launch quote are sourced from supplier documents. Growth, take-up, drop-off and the mix of tiers people buy are informed estimates with no measurement behind them, and they drive what a customer is worth. No price elasticity has ever been measured for this product, so every pricing result here is a tolerance, not a forecast.
